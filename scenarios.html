Cross-Scenario Findings

01
Rota Architecture
The 10-position rotating template with dedicated Flex slot is fundamentally sound. In all 20 scenarios, core services (NICU, SCBU, Nights) remained covered. The template never failed — people and processes around it did.
02
Flex Slot Effectiveness
The Flex slot absorbed the first-line gap in 18 of 20 scenarios. It failed only when (a) the Flex person was themselves the absent resident (Scenarios 3, 18), or (b) the Flex was already committed to an AL gap and a second absence occurred simultaneously (Scenarios 17, 20).
03
Rf Complement Criticality
Scenarios with 2 RFs (vs 3) consistently rated one tier worse in resilience. The goodwill reserve with 2 RFs is exhausted 40% faster under stress events. RF3 vacancy is not a minor administrative gap — it is a structural safety risk.
04
The Hard Floor
Every scenario identified a "hard floor" — the point at which one more person saying "no" would cause a genuine service gap. This floor is consistently reached at 4 simultaneous absences including at least one nights position. The rota cannot safely absorb more than this without external intervention.
05
Nights Are Always The Critical Variable
In 14 of 20 scenarios, the cover challenge that required the most creative solution was nights (weekday or weekend). Standard day shifts can be reduced, split, or supervised-down. Nights cannot. Nights gap = patient safety risk.
06
Administrative Failure Is As Dangerous As Clinical Failure
Scenarios 5, 10, 16, 18, and 19 demonstrate that administrative errors (late forms, timestamp failures, approval errors, opacity of Flex rules) create crises that clinical competence cannot easily fix. Process is not bureaucracy — it is safety infrastructure.
07
Goodwill Is A Finite Resource
RF goodwill (5 shifts/person), resident voluntary swaps, and REST-day cover are all finite. Once exhausted, the rota has no buffer. In Scenarios 9, 14, 17, and 20, goodwill reserve approached zero. Managing its depletion rate is as important as managing leave.
08
Wellbeing Is A Rota Issue
Scenarios 6, 12, 15, 19, and 20 all feature resident mental health or wellbeing as a driver of rota disruption. In no case was the crisis predictable from the rota alone — but in all cases, earlier wellbeing intervention would have reduced rota impact.
09
Team Cohesion Multiplies Resilience
The Dream Team (Scenario 1) absorbs events that would break Scenario 6 (Toxic Dynamic). The difference is not clinical skill — it is the informal goodwill of voluntary cover, proactive communication, and mutual trust. This cannot be mandated but can be cultivated.
10
Locum Pathway Speed Is Critical
In Scenario 4 (long-term sick) and Scenario 14 (RTA), the rota stress severity was directly proportional to locum response time. A 3-week locum delay = 3 weeks of maximum-stretch cover. Every department should have a pre-established agency relationship and credentialling pathway for emergency neonatal locums.
