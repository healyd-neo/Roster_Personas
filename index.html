Ten Things the
Stress Test Found

Distilled from 20 scenarios, 109 characters, and 23 weeks of modelled operational pressure.

01 — Architecture
The template is sound. Everything around it isn't always.
The 10-position rotating template with Flex slot held in all 20 scenarios. What failed were the processes, people, and buffers surrounding it.
02 — Flex Slot
The Flex slot is the rota's immune system.
It absorbed the first-line gap in 18 of 20 scenarios. It failed only when the Flex person was themselves absent, or already committed when a second absence hit.
03 — Research Fellows
Two RFs vs three is a tier-1 resilience difference.
Every 2-RF scenario rated one tier worse than its 3-RF equivalent. The RF3 vacancy is not an administrative inconvenience — it is a structural patient safety risk.
04 — Nights
Nights are always the hard problem.
In 14 of 20 scenarios, the nights shift was the hardest to cover. Day shifts can be reduced or supervised down. Nights cannot. Every nights gap is a patient safety event.
05 — Hard Floor
The hard floor is 4 simultaneous absences with nights exposed.
This limit appeared consistently. Beyond it, the rota requires external intervention. There is no internal solution.
06 — Goodwill
Goodwill is finite and must be actively managed.
RF goodwill (5 shifts each), voluntary swaps, and REST-day cover are consumed and depleted. Managing depletion rate matters as much as managing leave.
07 — Administration
Administrative failure is as dangerous as clinical failure.
Late forms, timestamping failures, approval errors, and opaque rules create crises that clinical competence cannot fix. Process is safety infrastructure.
08 — Locums
Locum pathway speed is everything in long-term sick.
Rota stress severity was directly proportional to locum response time. Every department should have a pre-established agency relationship before the crisis starts.
09 — Team Cohesion
Team cohesion is an invisible but critical variable.
The Dream Team absorbed events that broke the Toxic Dynamic. The difference was not clinical skill — it was trust and voluntary cover culture. It cannot be mandated but it can be cultivated.
10 — Wellbeing
Wellbeing events are rota events.
Burnout, AWOL, and mental health crises drove rota disruption in five scenarios. The absence of structured wellbeing monitoring is a gap in the rota system, not outside it.
